Business Proposal — Round 2

Accenture Innovation Challenge 2026 · Problem Track 4

Team Twin Flow — Aditya Singh · Divyansh Singh Mertia · Harshada Rajhans (IIT Kanpur)

1. Executive Summary

Large automotive assembly plants lose an average of ~27 hours per month to unplanned downtime, at a cost of up to $2.3M per hour (Siemens, The True Cost of Downtime, 2024). The root cause is not a lack of data but a lack of foresight: today's monitoring is reactive, so small deviations go undetected until they cascade into bottlenecks or ship as defects.

DigitalTwin.ai is a live, confidence-aware digital twin of the assembly line. It senses what is actually happening on the floor — including at legacy or manually-checked stations with little or no instrumentation — predicts where bottlenecks and defects will emerge before they happen, and recommends the specific action to take (reroute, rebalance, or root-cause) before losses accumulate.

This document covers the business case, target users, phased rollout and risk mitigations for the Round 2 prototype, built and validated on a simulated 40-station mixed-model line spanning body construction, paint, and final assembly.

2. Problem Statement

2.1 The core pain points

Delays cascade — minor cycle-time deviations at one station accumulate into bottlenecks, starving downstream stations and disrupting flow.

Defects propagate — a process deviation can pass through multiple stations before inspection catches the resulting defect, driving rework and scrap.

Data blind spots — legacy and manual stations lack continuous sensing, so early warning signals stay invisible until failure occurs.

Time & energy drain — equipment consumes energy and operators spend time waiting, monitoring, and correcting problems that could have been predicted.

2.2 Why this is harder than it looks

Round 2 scoping surfaced complexities that a naive dashboard cannot address, which directly shaped our design choices:

Sensor coverage is inherently uneven — some stations are richly instrumented, others rely entirely on manual checklists.

Bottlenecks and defects are often multi-causal and intermittent (equipment wear, operator variation, upstream part quality, environment), hard to isolate from data alone.

Live production systems (PLCs, line control) cannot be modified freely — most plants only allow retrofits during scheduled, infrequent maintenance windows.

A defect introduced early may not surface until a much later inspection point, by which time many downstream units may carry the same undetected issue.

Different stakeholders need different views of the same twin — a floor supervisor needs real-time signals, a plant manager needs weekly trends, leadership needs a business case.

Predictive claims must be validated against real outcomes over time — false alarms erode floor-level trust quickly.

3. Solution Overview

DigitalTwin.ai extends the classic Observe → Understand → Act framework into three engineered layers, with a confidence signal running through all three so users always know when to trust a number versus verify it themselves.

SENSE — ingest PLC/MES tags, vision frames, and historical production data. Where instrumentation is thin, a virtual-sensor layer infers station state from neighboring stations, timestamps, and computer vision — every estimate carries an explicit confidence score.

PREDICT — a station-level risk model (trained on historical/simulated deviations) scores bottleneck and defect likelihood per station. A graph-propagation layer models how risk and buffer starvation would ripple downstream, producing a time-to-impact estimate — not just “something is wrong” but “what happens next, and when.”

ACT — a rule-driven recommendation engine turns each prediction into a ranked, concrete action (reroute AGVs, rebalance labor, root-cause checklist) with an expected impact estimate, logged for supervisor review and override.

This is the same Observe / Understand / Act shape validated in our Round 1 concept, now made concrete enough to prototype: every module maps to a specific model, data structure, and UI surface (see the companion Technical PRD).

5. Business Case & Impact

We deliberately avoid fabricating precise savings for a specific, unnamed plant. Instead we model impact from the stated industry baseline and state our assumptions plainly, as Round 2 guidance asks:

Baseline: ~27 hours/month of unplanned downtime per plant, at up to $2.3M/hour (Siemens, 2024) — a monthly exposure in the tens of millions of dollars for a single large plant.

Illustrative target: even a conservative 15–30% reduction in unplanned downtime, driven by earlier detection and faster root-causing, represents a multi-million-dollar monthly swing per plant — the exact figure depends on a given plant's real cost stack, which we do not have access to.

Secondary savings: reduced rework/scrap from catching defects closer to origin, lower idle-machine energy spend, and better labor utilization from data-driven rebalancing instead of ad hoc firefighting.

The prototype's job is to demonstrate the predictive and recommendation mechanism convincingly on simulated data; the ROI model above is the framework a real pilot would populate with a plant's actual downtime and cost figures.

7. Phased Roadmap

Phase 0 — Now: Prototype validated on a simulated 40-station line (this submission).

Phase 1 (0–3 months): Shadow-mode pilot on one real line at one plant — recommendations are shown, not auto-executed; predictions are scored against real outcomes to earn trust.

Phase 2 (3–9 months): Closed-loop pilot (accepted recommendations acted on directly where safe), full-plant rollout across multiple lines, feedback loop wired in to retrain the risk model on real overrides.

Phase 3 (9–18 months): Multi-plant rollout using a topology-as-config onboarding kit so each new plant's differing layout, equipment vintage, and sensor maturity is a configuration exercise, not a rebuild; leadership-level cross-plant ROI reporting.

10. Stated Assumptions

Mixed-model line with 40 stations across Body Construction, Paint, and Final Assembly.

~80% of stations are richly instrumented; ~20% rely on manual checklists — used to demonstrate the virtual-sensing and confidence mechanism.

Instrumentation changes are only possible during scheduled maintenance windows (a small number per year).

All data used in the prototype is synthetic and simulated; no proprietary company data was used or required, per the challenge brief.

These parameters are directional and were chosen to match the Round 2 reference parameters for Track 4; they are configurable, not load-bearing assumptions baked into the model.
